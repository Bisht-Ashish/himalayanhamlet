Himalayan Hamlet / Almora guide

Working from the mountains

We work from this house ourselves, often. This is what it is really like: the internet, the quiet, and what a work day looks like with the Himalayas outside.

The connection

  • Airtel broadband, 100 Mbps, with a BSNL line as backup.
  • An inverter keeps the Wi-Fi running. Power cuts are rare, and mostly happen in bad weather.
  • Mobile networks work well, both Jio and Airtel.
  • It has been reliable. The only interruptions we see are during very heavy rain.
“For anyone wondering about work-from-home facilities, they have good network connectivity and reliable high-speed Wi-Fi. I was able to complete my office work seamlessly and attend Zoom calls without any glitches.”Sakshi, guest

Where you will work

Both bedrooms have a work desk set up. Many guests prefer the balcony instead, with the morning sun and the pleasant evenings.

It is quiet during the day. You hear birds, the wind moving through the dense forest nearby, and now and then the sun warming the tin roof. Sometimes the dogs are playing around outside.

“Great Wifi. Awesome home cooked meals. Very quiet and private. Himalayan views. Well equipped kitchen. Power Backup.”Vidit, guest

A work day here

This is how I spend my own days when I work from the house. It is a slow life, and you have time for yourself, so it is easy to focus.

  • Work with the morning sun on the balcony, and take breaks looking at the open valley and the snow-capped peaks.
  • Have home-cooked food instead of the office cafeteria, and say hello to the familiar faces around.
  • Walk around the house, play with the dogs, help my parents with small things, and have long conversations with them.
  • Between calls, water the plants and help in the kitchen garden, which is a good way to rest your eyes.
  • In the evening, go out for a walk. Kasar Devi is about a 10 minute drive, where you can sit in a cafe and watch the sunset with an even more open view of the peaks.
  • In winter, finish the day with a bonfire.

Staying longer

  • We offer longer stays depending on availability, with weekly and monthly discounts. Message us with your dates for the rates.
  • Meals are not part of the long-stay rate. The kitchen is well equipped, so many guests cook for themselves.
  • There is an automatic washing machine, and guests do their own laundry.
  • A grocery store is about 500 metres away on the main road with most daily needs. Local farmers also pass by with fresh vegetables from their fields, and you can buy from them directly. Milk can be arranged from the village, and the nearby shop will deliver groceries to the house.
“If you are looking for a long term stay and an abode away from the world, this is the place for you!”Sanjivani, guest

The longest stay: six weeks

A solo traveller came to explore on her own, and stayed six weeks.

  • 6 weeks
  • Holi with the neighbours
  • Joined by her mother, a friend and her husband

She spent her days at the slow pace of the town. She visited the temples around, some that even I did not know. She learned about spirituality, wrote a diary of everything, and got to know the neighbours. In the evenings we shared tea and long talks, and she cooked with the family and came along with us to the cafes. She celebrated Holi here.

After a few weeks her mother came to stay for a week. Then a friend joined her, and finally her husband. She is fun and full of life, and the house was happier for it.

“Brightly lit airy rooms that make it perfect for relaxation, positivity, study, and spiritual practice.”Guest, six-week stay
